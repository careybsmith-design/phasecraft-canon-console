# THREAD — narrated, step-by-step video training series

## Purpose
Create a practical training library that teaches new users **why to use THREAD, what to do next, exactly where to click, and how to verify the result**. Follow the Simple User Guide, correcting its gaps against the actual application before filming.

Use original, 3Blue1Brown-inspired visual explanations: progressive diagrams, connected objects, cause-and-effect animations and restrained text. Combine those explanations with readable screenshots of THREAD and an original ElevenLabs narrator—not an imitation of the creator's voice or branding.

**Recommended scope:** 15 chaptered lessons, approximately **97 minutes** in total. Short chapters make the library usable both as a course and as a reference. Produce one pilot first; approve its clarity, voice and pacing before producing the remaining lessons. No app changes, deployment or videos are part of this planning step.

## Teaching approach
- One continuing worked example: **reduce internal service-request turnaround from nine days to two**. These are proposed fictional training values, not company facts or demonstrated results. Label examples as training throughout.
- Follow the same Signal, Mission, session, confirmed objects and evidence through the course rather than introducing unrelated examples.
- Each action uses **Why → Where → What to enter → What to click → What success looks like**.
- Show the full journey once, then teach specialist tools when the learner actually needs them. Do not start with a long catalogue of technical terms.
- Explain new terms in everyday language before showing the THREAD term.
- Every lesson starts with prerequisites and ends with a visible result, a small practice task and the next lesson.
- Show important failure/recovery cases: unsaved entry, duplicate signal, missing promotion, wrong decision authority, held-back attachments, incomplete evidence and unavailable external services.

## Curriculum and walkthroughs

| # | Lesson / estimated length | Screen-by-screen sequence and learner outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THREAD: what it does and why it matters** · 4 min | Start with scattered notes and unanswered “why?” questions. Animate Signal → Mission → Cell/Burst → Framing → confirmed intent → reuse/build → trial → evidence. Show Feed and a connected example. End with a clear distinction between a problem, a goal, a working session and proof of a result. |
| 2 | **Find your way around THREAD** · 6 min | Tour the actual menu, grouped by purpose without changing its order. Demonstrate Feed, Missions, Framing and Decisions as everyday destinations; Fabric, Creation, Shadow, Generation, Measurement and Evidence as delivery/checking; Canon, Graph, Authority, Assurance, Detection, Agents and Simulation as specialist tools. Show how Feed/Missions lead to Signals and how the compass opens Value Companion. End with “where do I go for this task?” exercises. |
| 3 | **Record and promote a Value Signal** · 7 min | Feed → Record a signal, and Missions → Record a new signal. Demonstrate Record directly → observed condition → Save signal → saved reference → reload/register read-back. Demonstrate Typed capture → Complete capture and review signals → Confirm and save signal. Cover upload intake separately from framing uploads, duplicates, and available capture channels. Promote with a rationale using an authorized participant; show a suggestion-only outcome for someone without authority. End by proving a saved but unpromoted signal is not a mission source. |
| 4 | **Create a measurable Value Mission** · 7 min | Missions → Source Value Signal → select the promoted signal. Enter condition, baseline, target, affected population, boundaries, required proof, investment limit, review date, authority and stop conditions. Choose two different people as Co-Creator and Builder. Save and reopen the mission; verify its originating signal and bounds. Cover the empty source list and missing participant recovery cases. |
| 5 | **Form a Value Cell and run a Value Burst** · 5 min | Open the mission → Form Value Cell → choose eligible participants/agents where supported → set Burst duration → Begin Value Burst. Explain the seven phases as a focused working cycle, not a status meeting. Finish with a Substantive output reference and rationale; inspect its recorded output. Explain that elapsed time alone does not prove delivery. |
| 6 | **Prepare and open a Framing session** · 6 min | Framing → start a session → select participants and speaker/decision roles → optionally associate the mission → obtain required consent → Open capture. Demonstrate account-holder consent versus attestation for non-account participants. Show where session-to-mission association can be made later, who may do it and how reversal preserves earlier confirmations. Mission association is optional; mission creation still needs a promoted signal. |
| 7 | **Capture conversations, notes and artifacts** · 8 min | In the same session, enter typed notes and inspect Live extraction; demonstrate actual live speech only if connected and working, explicitly distinguish simulated transcription. Add supported text and a screenshot, then inspect its capture/read result. Show available Outlook/Teams paths only after connection verification. Demonstrate blocked/held-back content and the visible recovery action. End with source captures and extracted drafts—not yet agreed requirements. |
| 8 | **Review, clarify and confirm what was meant** · 8 min | Follow a draft from source text to its classified object. Review/correct wording through supported controls; distinguish needs, behaviours, assumptions, questions and decisions. Demonstrate authorized Confirm and disabled/missing-authority outcomes. Resolve Live prompts, Material items and contradictions, then close the session and inspect its completeness result. Verify confirmed objects and their provenance; do not imply every object is a requirement or every closure passes. |
| 9 | **Graph essentials: see the story, not a tangle** · 7 min | Graph → Mission Constellation → choose the training mission → select a node and inspect details → Focus. Teach nodes, relationship labels/direction, filters, zoom and navigation using actual available controls. Compare the visual map with the mission and evidence views. End by finding a need, its behaviour and the supporting source without browsing every object. |
| 10 | **Graph investigations: dependencies, gaps and change impact** · 8 min | Answer practical questions using Need Web, Dependency Web, Decomposition Tree and Contradiction Map. Use Path Finder for connections and Impact / Blast Radius before a proposed change. Introduce Canon Navigator, Nebula and Graph integrity; explain family, authority, expiration and detector overlays. Read edge limits and inferred versus authoritative relationships honestly. Finish with a short “what changes if this changes?” investigation; graph impact is not itself approval. |
| 11 | **Reuse first, then prepare a build** · 7 min | Fabric → search the confirmed need → inspect candidate evidence → Record reuse check → show reuse/compose/new-build branches. Creation → choose confirmed behaviour statements, outcome contract, evaluated template, reuse check and target platform → Compile prompt → inspect prompt and dispatch/build records. Clearly distinguish compiling, requesting an external build and receiving an evidenced result. Simulated runs stay visibly simulated. |
| 12 | **Test safely, authorize exposure and retain a way back** · 7 min | Shadow → inspect/record shadow evidence → discrepancies → Progressive exposure → required authority → Authorise exposure → inspect rollback/kill-switch controls. Explain independent review, population bounds, expiry and stop conditions. Demonstrate live operation only when the external integration and evidence support it; otherwise show the actual recorded/simulated workflow, not a fictional deployment. |
| 13 | **Check value, explain results and generate useful documents** · 6 min | Mission → Production evidence and confidence → Measurement → Median intent-to-value time and evidence-linked families. Interpret No evidence/Not measured honestly. Evidence → Explain an object → nine questions → traceability queries. Generation → select supported sources/template → generate and review a cited rendition. A generated document is a presentation of evidence, not a new authoritative requirement or proof of value. |
| 14 | **Make decisions and keep the system trustworthy** · 7 min | Decisions → Human Decision Queue → inspect evidence → take the implemented authority-appropriate response. Canon → propose a change → challenge window → steward decision. Authority → inspect boundaries; Detection → investigate a finding; Assurance → read checks and unresolved live evidence. Agents → explain connections within the same boundaries. Distinguish a recorded decision from execution in an external system. |
| 15 | **Full end-to-end walkthrough and guided practice** · 4 min | Revisit the linked case in order: saved/promoted signal → bounded mission → Cell/Burst → consented framing → confirmed intent → graph check → reuse → build evidence → controlled trial → measured outcome. Use references to earlier chapters rather than repeating every field. Mark any simulated or unavailable stage. End with a pause-and-practise checklist, Simulation, and Value Companion for next-step help. |

### Suggested viewing paths
- **New user:** 1–2, then 3–10 in order; 15 as a recap.
- **Business Co-Creator / decision owner:** 1–10, 13–15.
- **Builder:** 1–13 and 15; consult 14 for governance.
- **Steward / reviewer:** 1–2, 8–10 and 12–15.
All menu destinations receive an introduction; substantial workflows receive dedicated demonstrations rather than token mentions.

## Screenshot and visual plan
- Capture **the actual application build used for training**, not design-proposal images or invented UI. Existing desktop/mobile proposals are visual references only.
- For each procedure capture: destination, completed input before submission, saved result, reopened result, and any teaching-relevant error/authority state. Use cropped close-ups and a highlighted pointer so labels remain readable.
- Use a consistent screenshot inventory keyed to lesson, step, record reference, app version and date. Hide personal identifiers and secrets; keep meaningful references consistent across lessons.
- Prefer a populated, owner-approved training environment with two genuine participant roles. Do not weaken protections or fabricate evidence to populate scenes. If training records must be added to the main environment, agree their labelling and cleanup first.
- Show the current test-mode banner honestly if filming that build; mark it as build-phase access, not the final sign-in procedure.
- Match THREAD's charcoal/graphite, restrained gold, semantic status colours and typography. Animate the current object/relationship into its screenshot location; use colour plus labels, never colour alone.
- Planned mix: roughly two-thirds practical screenshot walkthrough and one-third diagrams, adjusted per lesson. Calm transitions; pause long enough to read entries and saved references.
- Default delivery: 16:9, 1920×1080 MP4 with narration, chapter timestamps, separate captions and transcript. Avoid tiny whole-screen recordings and music underneath detailed instructions.

## Production and approval sequence
1. **Approve this curriculum.** Confirm the proposed example and scope; no need to decide technical tools.
2. **Verify teaching truth.** Walk the actual controls, reconcile guide wording, inventory screenshots and classify every stage as demonstrated live, simulated, or unavailable. Do not silently edit the existing guide.
3. **Prepare the training case and narrator.** Establish participant access and required evidence; connect ElevenLabs and review the short voice sample.
4. **Produce pilot lesson 3.** It tests diagrams, screenshots, narration, recovery cases and saved-result verification around the user's previously confusing signal workflow.
5. **Review the rendered pilot.** Assess whether a beginner can repeat the task unaided. Refine narration, readability and pacing before committing to the full set.
6. **Produce in batches:** orientation/Mission (1–5), Framing/Graph (6–10), delivery/governance/recap (11–15). Review each batch before proceeding.
7. **Deliver the library.** Numbered videos, captions, transcripts, checklists and a learning-path index in Files. An in-app video library is not included unless separately requested.

## Accuracy checks and known dependencies
- Source review found guide gaps: missing Cell/Burst steps, an inaccurate strictly linear Framing prerequisite, oversimplified consent, and build/trial/measurement descriptions that imply more than the UI proves. Resolve these in the scripts.
- Mission sources remain promoted-only; machine suggestions remain distinct from human confirmations. Use two different people for mission roles; do not invent a second person's authority.
- Documents without approved extraction remain held back. Confirm image-reading clearance and actual text/speech/Microsoft availability at filming time, not from old screenshots.
- A recorded shadow run, selected “live” mode, compiled prompt or approved decision does not establish that an external trial, deployment or measured result occurred. Label simulated examples and unverified adapters explicitly.
- Verify every narrated click against the captured UI and every claimed save by reopening the record. Verify graph relationships against the same records.
- Inspect each rendered lesson for clipping, unreadable screenshots, incorrect highlighting, audio gaps, caption drift and misleading status changes. If motion review is incomplete, deliver a review draft rather than claim it is final.
- Maintain a course coverage checklist: every menu, guide workflow, prerequisite, success state and recovery case. Keep blocked live demonstrations open rather than manufacture evidence.
